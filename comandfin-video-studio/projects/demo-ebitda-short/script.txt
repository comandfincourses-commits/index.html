Ninety percent of students get EBITDA wrong in interviews. Let's fix that in under a minute.
EBITDA stands for Earnings Before Interest, Taxes, Depreciation and Amortisation.
In simple words, it is the profit a business makes from its core operations, before financing decisions, tax and non cash charges.
Here is the quick formula. Take net profit, add back interest, tax, depreciation and amortisation.
Example. Net profit is forty crore. Interest is ten, tax is fifteen, and depreciation is twenty five. So EBITDA is ninety crore.
Why do analysts love it? Because it lets you compare companies with different debt levels and tax rates.
But remember, EBITDA is not cash flow. It ignores working capital and capital expenditure.
