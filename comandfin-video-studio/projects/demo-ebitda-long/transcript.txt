[  21.66 –   36.29] Let's begin with the definition. EBITDA stands for Earnings Before Interest, Taxes, Depreciation and amortisation. It measures the profit generated by the core operations of a business before the effects of financing decisions, tax rules and non-cash accounting charges.
[  36.66 –   53.57] There are two ways to calculate it. The top-down method starts with revenue and subtracts operating expenses like raw material, employee costs, and other expenses. The bottom-up method starts with net profit and adds back interest, tax, depreciation, and amortisation. Both methods must give you the same answer.
[  54.04 –   64.13] Let's take an example. A company reports revenue of ₹500 crore. Operating expenses are ₹410 crore. So, EBITDA is ₹90 crore, and the EBITDA margin is 18%.
[  64.54 –   74.37] Now check it bottom up. Net profit is ₹40 crore. Add back tax of ₹15 crore. Interest of ₹10 crore. And depreciation of ₹25 crore. Once again, EBITDA is ₹90 crore.
[  74.84 –   94.40] So where is it used? First, valuation. Bankers apply an EV/EBITDA multiple. If peers trade at 10 times. This company may be worth around ₹900 crore of enterprise value. Second, credit analysis. Banks look at net debt to EBITDA to decide how much a company can borrow. Third, performance comparison across companies with different capital structures.
[  94.81 –  109.92] Now the common mistakes. Mistake 1, treating EBITDA as cash flow. It ignores working capital and capital expenditure. Mistake 2, comparing EBITDA margins across very different industries. Mistake 3, ignoring one-time items. Always adjust for exceptional gains or losses.
[ 110.33 –  122.37] Here is a classic interview question. Can a company have positive EBITDA and still go bankrupt? The answer is yes. If debt repayments, interest and capital expenditure exceed the cash generated, the company can still run out of money.
[ 122.81 –  131.94] To summarise, EBITDA shows operating profitability, it helps compare companies and it drives valuation and lending decisions, but always pair it with cash flow analysis.
