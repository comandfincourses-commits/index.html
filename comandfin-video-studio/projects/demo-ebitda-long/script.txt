If you are preparing for a finance interview, there is one metric you simply cannot avoid. EBITDA. Investment bankers use it to value companies, lenders use it to set loan limits, and equity analysts quote it in every earnings call. Yet most students can only recite the full form. In this video, you will learn what EBITDA really means, how to calculate it, where it is used in the industry, and the mistakes that can cost you an interview.
Let's begin with the definition. EBITDA stands for Earnings Before Interest, Taxes, Depreciation and Amortisation. It measures the profit generated by the core operations of a business, before the effects of financing decisions, tax rules, and non cash accounting charges.
There are two ways to calculate it. The top down method starts with revenue, and subtracts operating expenses like raw material, employee cost and other expenses. The bottom up method starts with net profit, and adds back interest, tax, depreciation and amortisation. Both methods must give you the same answer.
Let's take an example. A company reports revenue of five hundred crore. Operating expenses are four hundred and ten crore. So EBITDA is ninety crore, and the EBITDA margin is eighteen percent.
Now check it bottom up. Net profit is forty crore. Add back tax of fifteen crore, interest of ten crore, and depreciation of twenty five crore. Once again, EBITDA is ninety crore.
So where is it used? First, valuation. Bankers apply an EV to EBITDA multiple. If peers trade at ten times, this company may be worth around nine hundred crore of enterprise value. Second, credit analysis. Banks look at net debt to EBITDA to decide how much a company can borrow. Third, performance comparison across companies with different capital structures.
Now the common mistakes. Mistake one, treating EBITDA as cash flow. It ignores working capital and capital expenditure. Mistake two, comparing EBITDA margins across very different industries. Mistake three, ignoring one time items. Always adjust for exceptional gains or losses.
Here is a classic interview question. Can a company have positive EBITDA and still go bankrupt? The answer is yes. If debt repayments, interest and capital expenditure exceed the cash generated, the company can still run out of money.
To summarise. EBITDA shows operating profitability, it helps compare companies, and it drives valuation and lending decisions. But always pair it with cash flow analysis.
