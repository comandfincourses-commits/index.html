## Voiceover only

Same biryani. Same app. So why is one three hundred and fifty rupees, and the one below it two hundred?
Meet Riya. She's hungry. She opens the app and scrolls. Lucknowi Dum House. Biryani Junction. Hyderabadi Handi.
All biryani. But not the same biryani.
That's monopolistic competition. Many sellers, each with a slightly different product.
Four features. One: lots of competitors.
Two: easy to enter and exit. A cloud kitchen can go live in weeks.
Three: products are differentiated, so each seller has some power over price.
Four: they compete on more than price. Ratings, food photos, delivery time, a bestseller tag, combos and ads.
Hyderabadi Handi launches a smoky dum biryani at a premium. Orders jump. Profits rise.
But profits attract copycats. Within months, two new kitchens offer smoky biryani for less, and that extra profit shrinks.
That's the long run: differentiation earns profit for a while, and new entry competes it away.
Recap: many sellers, easy entry, a lot of differentiation and advertising, some control over price.
You see it in restaurants, salons, coaching classes and clothing brands.
Interview tip: the demand curve slopes down, but it's elastic, because substitutes are one scroll away.
Next: oligopoly, when only a few big players run the show. Follow COMandFIN.
