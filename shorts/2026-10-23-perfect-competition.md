## Voiceover only

Why can't this wheat seller charge even one rupee more?
Meet three sellers at a wheat mandi. Sharma ji, Kaur ji and Patel ji. Same wheat. Same quality. Same price: twenty-five rupees a kilo.
Now Sharma ji tries twenty-six. Watch what happens. Every buyer walks to the next stall. Not one comes back.
That's perfect competition.
It has four features. One: many sellers, each too small to move the market.
Two: identical products. Wheat is wheat.
Three: easy entry and exit. Any farmer can bring a cart tomorrow.
Four: so no seller controls the price. They are price takers. Demand and supply set it for the whole market.
So how much should Sharma ji sell? He keeps selling until the cost of one more kilo, his marginal cost, equals the market price.
And in the long run? High profits pull in new farmers. Supply rises, and profits shrink back to normal.
Real life comes close in farm mandis and stock markets, where thousands trade the same share.
Recap: many sellers, no differentiation, no price control, easy entry.
Interview tip: here, a firm's demand curve is a flat line.
