Meet Rahul. He just got his first salary of fifty thousand rupees. And like most of us, he has one question. Should I spend it, or should I invest it?
Here's the deal. Money can grow in two ways. Simple interest, and compound interest.
With simple interest, you earn returns only on what you put in. With compound interest, you earn returns on your returns.
Let's say Rahul invests one lakh rupees at twelve percent a year.
After ten years, simple interest gives him two lakh twenty thousand rupees.
But compounding? It gives him three lakh ten thousand rupees. Same money. Same rate. Very different result.
And the real magic is time. Start at twenty five instead of thirty five, and your money has ten extra years to snowball.
So the lesson is simple. Start early. Stay invested. And let compounding do the heavy lifting.
