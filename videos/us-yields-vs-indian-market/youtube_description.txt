₹52,000 crore pulled out of Indian stocks in just two weeks, and the reason isn't Sensex, RBI or even India. It's an interest rate on a US government bond. 🇺🇸📉

In this video, CA Ankit Bajaj explains how the bond market quietly controls the stock market, why foreign investors (FIIs) sell Indian shares when US yields rise, and the No. 1 mistake retail investors make when they hear "FIIs are selling".

✅ What You Will Learn
• The seesaw rule: why bond prices fall when interest rates rise (₹1,000 bond at 7% → ~₹933 when rates hit 8%)
• 3 ways rising bond yields pull share prices down: competition, valuation and borrowing cost
• Why a fund manager in New York sells Indian shares when the US-India yield gap shrinks
• The vicious circle: FII outflows → stronger dollar → weaker rupee → more outflows
• Why you should check the US 10-year Treasury yield before you panic-sell
• An interview-ready answer: how rising US yields affect emerging markets like India
