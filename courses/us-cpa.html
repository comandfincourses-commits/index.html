About the program

What is US CPA?

The Uniform CPA Examination is developed by the AICPA and administered with NASBA and Prometric. Under the CPA Evolution model, candidates pass three Core sections and one Discipline section of their choice. Licensing is granted by individual US state boards.

Who should join

  • B.Com / M.Com graduates and final-year students
  • Qualified or pursuing CAs looking for a US credential
  • Audit, accounting and tax professionals in Big 4 and MNCs
  • Professionals working on US clients or US GAAP reporting

What you will be able to do

  • Apply US GAAP and governmental accounting to complex transactions
  • Plan and perform audit and attestation engagements
  • Understand US federal taxation, business law and ethics
  • Solve task-based simulations with exam-level speed and accuracy
Curriculum

Program structure

Core — FAR: Financial Accounting and ReportingCore
  • Conceptual framework and financial statements
  • Select transactions and US GAAP topics
  • State and local government accounting
  • Not-for-profit reporting
Core — AUD: Auditing and AttestationCore
  • Ethics, professional responsibilities and general principles
  • Assessing risk and developing a planned response
  • Performing procedures and obtaining evidence
  • Forming conclusions and reporting
Core — REG: Taxation and RegulationCore
  • Ethics, professional responsibilities and federal tax procedures
  • Business law
  • Federal taxation of property transactions
  • Federal taxation of individuals and entities
Discipline (choose one) — BAR / ISC / TCPDiscipline
  • BAR: Business Analysis and Reporting
  • ISC: Information Systems and Controls
  • TCP: Tax Compliance and Planning
  • Counselling to choose the discipline that fits your career

Exam structure reflects the CPA Evolution model introduced in January 2024. Eligibility and licensing rules vary by state board; verify the latest on aicpa-cima.com and nasba.org.
